Gup Shup

Kids build real Punjabi fluency by talking — in small groups of friends they already know, for 15 minutes at a time.

The problem isn't vocabulary. It's peer-shame.

Most diaspora kids understand Punjabi and will speak it with parents or grandparents. Almost none will speak it with other kids. Duolingo-style apps drill words a child already half-knows — they don't touch the actual barrier, which is social, not linguistic.

Kids don't need another app that teaches them words.
They need a room where speaking Punjabi with their friends is normal.

Anatomy of a session — 15 minutes, invite-only

Opening ritual2 min
Topic rounds9 min — 4 kids, ~2 min each
Group finale2 min
Closing ritual2 min

Same chant opens and closes every session — no improvising, no performance pressure. Private feedback note sent to each kid afterward.

ROADMAP

MVP → Iteration → North Star

NOW · MVP

Prove kids want this

  • Invite-only pods of 3–5 kids families already know
  • Video call + human host, printed/digital topic cards
  • Manual effort-based badges, no app build required
  • Pilot with families + one Punjabi school or Gurdwara class
NEXT · ITERATION

Make it a product

  • Native app: scheduling, timer, live badges, streaks
  • AI drafts topic cards and post-session feedback
  • AI flags language slips for the host to nudge — not autonomous
  • Cohorts get names/identity — "their" recurring group
LATER · NORTH STAR

Scale trust, not just tech

  • AI co-host handles live nudges; adult shifts to spot-checking
  • Community/school marketplace of vetted hosts
  • Kids run their own recurring "shows" with friends
  • Expand beyond Punjabi to other under-served mother tongues

Why this isn't Duolingo, and isn't Toastmasters

Duolingo — solo drills. No pressure, but no one to talk to.

Toastmasters / debate — social, but built to train performance under pressure. Assumes you're already fluent.

Gup Shup — social and low-stakes. A playdate where Punjabi is the shared game, not a craft being judged.

Safety model at launch

Invite-only pods

Kids only ever talk to peers their family already knows. No stranger matching.

Adult always present

A real host runs every session live — safety, energy, and language nudges.

Effort over accuracy

Attempts are rewarded before correctness. English fallback is never punished.

The ask

Pilot Gup Shup this season with 3–5 families and one community Punjabi class.
